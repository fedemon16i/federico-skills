# Interview Project Narrative (Verbal)

**Status:** Done · **Priority:** High  
**Use when:** “Tell me about a project” / walkthrough with PM or product designers.

---

## Goal

In ~5–10 minutes: prove **ownership, judgment, collaboration, and outcome** — not a tour of every Figma frame.

---

## Spine (memorize)

1. **Problem** — what was broken / opportunity  
2. **I owned** — scope boundary  
3. **What we knew** — data, research, constraints  
4. **What I found** — insight  
5. **Options** — alternatives  
6. **I recommended** — decision + why  
7. **With PM/eng** — how alignment worked  
8. **We shipped**  
9. **What happened** — outcome (honest)  
10. **What I’d change** — reflection  

Maps to STAR: Situation/Task ≈ 1–3 · Action ≈ 4–8 · Result ≈ 9–10.  
Senior filter: **I decided / I traded off / I owned the outcome** — not only “we shipped.”

---

## Decision cards (say 2–3 aloud)

For each: **Decision · Evidence · Alternative rejected · Trade-off · Impact**

Example: “Instrument before redesign vs redesign nav on a hunch — no event map existed, so we couldn’t see the real drop.”

---

## Data philosophy (keep this line)

If numbers go against the design, they’re not a personal attack — they show the solution isn’t working.  
Use in portfolio and interviews; it’s a seniority signal.

---

## Figma & AI positioning (from prep)

- Figma remains in the workflow; scope expanded upstream (discovery, behavioral analysis, systems).  
- AI = force multiplier across synthesis, alternatives, prototype, docs — **you stay accountable for decisions**.  
- Don’t list tools as the differentiator; show the loop.

---

## What interviewers probe

Alternatives, what you’d redo, disagreement with PM/eng, technical constraints, how success was measured, ownership vs team credit.

Prepare one story that hits **conflict/trade-off** and one that hits **measurement changed the design**.
