# Rapid Validation

**Status:** ADOPT  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-22  
**Sources:** Maze, Optimal Workshop, Usability.gov, practical product experience

---

## Purpose

How to generate useful research signal when time is extremely limited (1 day → 1 week).  
This is the default mode for most real product work.

---

## Core principle

> Perfect research that arrives too late is worse than good-enough research that arrives in time to influence the decision.

Always ask first:  
**What decision does this research need to unlock?**

---

## Validation windows

### 1 Day
**Goal:** Directional signal, not statistical confidence.

Possible activities:
- 3–5 unmoderated Maze tests (or similar)
- 2–3 quick stakeholder/user calls (15–20 min)
- First-click or preference test
- Review of existing session replays / analytics

Output: Clear recommendation + main risks + what still needs validation.

### 2–3 Days
**Goal:** Enough signal to de-risk a design direction or feature.

Possible activities:
- 5–8 unmoderated sessions
- 4–6 short interviews
- Tree test or card sort if information architecture is in question
- Basic funnel / path analysis from existing tools (Pendo, Mixpanel, etc.)

Output: Prioritized findings + recommended changes + confidence level.

### 1 Week
**Goal:** Solid enough to support a launch or major design decision.

Possible activities:
- 8–12 moderated or unmoderated sessions
- Mix of qualitative + quantitative
- Comparison of alternatives
- Clear success metrics defined in advance

---

## Minimum viable research design

Before starting any study, answer these four questions in writing:

1. What decision will this research influence?
2. What would make us change our mind?
3. What is the fastest way to get a trustworthy signal?
4. How will we capture and share the learning?

If you cannot answer these clearly, do not start the research yet.

---

## What can be delegated vs what needs judgment

| Can often be delegated | Needs Federico’s judgment |
|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Setting up unmoderated tests | Interpreting what the findings mean for the product |
| Recruiting participants | Deciding which findings are critical vs nice-to-have |
| Running basic analysis | Choosing the research method under time pressure |
| Creating the test script draft | Deciding when “good enough” is enough |

---

## Recommended tools (current)

- **Unmoderated testing:** Maze
- **Card sorting / Tree testing / First-click:** Optimal Workshop
- **Behavioral context:** Pendo / Mixpanel / Hotjar
- **Surveys:** Qualtrics (when needed)

---

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Running research without a clear decision in mind
- Waiting for “perfect” sample size when time is limited
- Treating every finding as equally important
- Not documenting the learning so it disappears after the project
