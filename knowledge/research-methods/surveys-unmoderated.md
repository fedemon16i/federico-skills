# Surveys & Unmoderated Research Methods

**Status:** Done · **Priority:** Medium  
**Use when:** Need signal without full moderated interviews; validate copy, IA, or satisfaction at light weight.

---

## When to use (and when not)

| Use | Avoid as only method |
|-----|----------------------|
| Preference between 2–3 clear options | Discovering *unknown* problems |
| Satisfaction / ease scores after a flow | Complex task understanding |
| Unmoderated prototype tasks (Maze-style) | Political stakeholder alignment |
| Quick pulse inside an existing product | Replacing all qualitative work |

Federico’s default world is **existing products** — unmoderated tests and in-product surveys often beat big discovery projects.

---

## Survey basics that don’t waste time

- One goal per survey
- Max ~5–7 questions for completion
- Prefer behavioral (“last time you…”) over hypothetical
- Scales: keep consistent; label endpoints
- Always allow “N/A / didn’t use”
- Close with optional open text (one box)

**Anti-pattern:** 25-question “UX survey” nobody finishes.

---

## Unmoderated usability (prototype or prod)

1. Define **task + success** (not “explore the app”)
2. Recruit users who match the job (or in-product intercept)
3. Tools: Maze, Useberry, Optimal Workshop (IA), or product-native guides
4. Sample: small but real (e.g. 5–15 for directional; more for metrics)
5. Capture: completion, time, misclicks, verbatim

Pair with **session replay** (Hotjar/Pendo/etc.) when available instead of inventing new tooling.

---

## Card sorts & tree tests (IA)

- **Card sort:** labels and grouping open/closed
- **Tree test:** “where would you find X?” on proposed structure
- Use when navigation or labels are the risk — not when the problem is performance or trust
