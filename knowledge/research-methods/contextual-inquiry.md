# Contextual Inquiry

**Status:** LEARN  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-22  
**Sources:** Classic HCI methods, Practical adaptation for remote/modern product work

---

## Purpose

Understand how people actually work in their real context by observing and interviewing them while they perform their tasks.

It is deeper than a standard interview because it combines observation + conversation in the moment.

---

## When it is worth doing

- Complex workflows that are hard to understand through interviews alone
- When users have developed strong workarounds
- Early in problem discovery for high-stakes domains
- When the gap between what people say and what they do is likely large

It is usually too heavy for rapid 1–3 day validation cycles.

---

## Core principles (classic)

1. **Context** — Go to the user’s environment (or recreate it as faithfully as possible)
2. **Partnership** — Treat the user as the expert; you are the apprentice
3. **Interpretation** — Jointly make sense of what is happening
4. **Focus** — Keep the inquiry tied to the research goals

---

## Modern / remote adaptation

Full in-person contextual inquiry is often impractical. Useful adaptations:

- Screen-sharing while the user performs real tasks + think-aloud
- Short remote sessions focused on one critical workflow
- Combining recorded sessions (Hotjar/Pendo/session replay) with follow-up interviews
- Diary-style or “show me how you did it last time” prompts

---

## Minimum viable version

1. Define the workflow or job-to-be-done you need to understand
2. Recruit 3–5 people who actually do that work
3. Observe them performing it (live or recorded) while asking clarifying questions
4. Capture tools used, sequence, pain points, workarounds, and decision points
5. Synthesize into a realistic picture of current behavior

---

## Anti-patterns

- Treating it as a long unstructured conversation
- Observing without a clear focus
- Not capturing the sequence and tools involved
- Using it when a lighter method would have been enough
