# Interviews (User + Stakeholder)

**Status:** ADOPT  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-22  
**Sources:** Practical product experience, Optimal Workshop, Usability.gov, IDEO (filtered)

---

## Purpose

Extract useful insights through conversation when you need depth that analytics and unmoderated tests cannot provide.

---

## Two main types

### 1. User Interviews
Goal: Understand behaviors, motivations, pain points, and mental models of actual or potential users.

### 2. Stakeholder Interviews
Goal: Align on goals, constraints, success criteria, and political/organizational reality.

Both are valuable. Mixing them without clarity creates confusion.

---

## When to use interviews

- Early discovery (problem space)
- Understanding “why” behind quantitative drop-offs
- Aligning stakeholders before a major initiative
- Validating assumptions that are too risky to leave unexamined

---

## Minimum viable interview approach

1. Write a clear research goal (what decision will this influence?)
2. Create a short discussion guide (not a rigid script)
3. Recruit 4–8 people from the right segment
4. Run 25–45 min sessions
5. Take structured notes (or record + summarize)
6. Synthesize into themes + implications for the product

---

## Discussion guide structure (recommended)

1. Warm-up / context (2–3 min)
2. Current behavior and context
3. Specific experiences (critical incidents)
4. Pain points and workarounds
5. Reactions to ideas / concepts (if applicable)
6. Closing / anything else

Keep most questions open. Avoid leading the participant.

---

## Synthesis tips

- Group findings into themes
- Separate observations from interpretations
- Always link insights back to the original decision
- Note confidence level (how many people said this, how strongly)

---

## Anti-patterns

- Treating interviews as a checkbox activity
- Asking only about opinions instead of past behavior
- Not synthesizing (just collecting quotes)
- Interviewing the wrong people
- Letting stakeholders turn user interviews into sales calls
